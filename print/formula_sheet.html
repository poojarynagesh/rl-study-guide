SituationUseWhere

General

NameFormulaWhen to useRef
Incremental rule$\textit{New}\leftarrow\textit{Old}+\textit{StepSize}\,[\textit{Target}-\textit{Old}]$Any running estimate: bandits, MC, TD, function approximation. Move the estimate a fraction of the way to a target.2.4
Step-size conditions$\sum_n\alpha_n=\infty,\quad\sum_n\alpha_n^2\lt\infty$Check convergence of a step-size schedule. 1/n passes; a constant α fails the 2nd condition (so it tracks change but never settles).2.7
Return$G_t=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\gamma^kR_{t+k+1}=R_{t+1}+\gamma G_{t+1}$Define what the agent maximises. Use $G_t=R_{t+1}+\gamma G_{t+1}$ to compute returns backwards through an episode.3.8, 3.9
Unified return$G_t=\sum_{k=t+1}^{T}\gamma^{k-t-1}R_k$Write episodic and continuing tasks in one formula ($T=\infty$ or $\gamma=1$, never both).3.11

Chapter 2 — Bandits

NameFormulaWhen to useRef
Action value$q_*(a)=\E[R_t\mid A_t=a]$Definition of an arm's true value; the quantity every bandit method tries to estimate.§2.1
Sample average$Q_t(a)=\dfrac{\sum_{i\lt t}R_i\mathbb 1_{A_i=a}}{\sum_{i\lt t}\mathbb 1_{A_i=a}}$Stationary bandit, estimating $Q(a)$ from observed rewards.2.1
Greedy$A_t=\argmax_aQ_t(a)$; ε-greedy picks greedy w.p. $1-\varepsilon+\varepsilon/k$Action selection. Pure greedy exploits only; use ε-greedy when you must keep exploring.2.2
Incremental mean$Q_{n+1}=Q_n+\frac1n[R_n-Q_n]$Compute the average in O(1) memory without storing past rewards.2.3
Constant α$Q_{n+1}=Q_n+\alpha[R_n-Q_n]=(1-\alpha)^nQ_1+\sum_{i=1}^n\alpha(1-\alpha)^{n-i}R_i$Non-stationary problems: recent rewards weigh more (exponential recency-weighted average).2.5, 2.6
UCB$A_t=\argmax_a\Big[Q_t(a)+c\sqrt{\ln t/N_t(a)}\Big]$Explore by uncertainty: rarely tried actions get a bonus. Stationary bandits; needs the count $N_t(a)$.2.10
Soft-max$\pi_t(a)=e^{H_t(a)}/\sum_be^{H_t(b)}$Turn action preferences $H$ into probabilities (gradient bandit policy).2.11
Gradient bandit$H_{t+1}(a)=H_t(a)+\alpha(R_t-\bar R_t)(\mathbb 1_{a=A_t}-\pi_t(a))$Learn preferences instead of values; subtract the baseline $\bar R_t$ to reduce variance. Rewards' absolute scale then does not matter.2.12

Chapter 3 — Finite MDPs

NameFormulaWhen to useRef
Dynamics$p(s',r\mid s,a)=\Pr\{S_t=s',R_t=r\mid S_{t-1}=s,A_{t-1}=a\}$Defines an MDP; every other quantity is derived from $p$. Use it when the model is known.3.2
Derived quantities$p(s'|s,a)=\sum_rp(s',r|s,a)$, $\ r(s,a)=\sum_rr\sum_{s'}p(s',r|s,a)$Marginalise $p$ to get transition probabilities or expected reward when the algorithm needs only these.3.4, 3.5
Constant reward$\sum_k\gamma^k=1/(1-\gamma)$Closed-form return of a continuing task with constant reward $r$: $r/(1-\gamma)$.3.10
State value$v_\pi(s)=\E_\pi[G_t\mid S_t=s]$Definition: expected return from $s$ following π. The target of prediction.3.12
Action value$q_\pi(s,a)=\E_\pi[G_t\mid S_t=s,A_t=a]$Definition: expected return after taking $a$ in $s$, then following π. Needed for control without a model.3.13
v ↔ q$v_\pi(s)=\sum_a\pi(a|s)q_\pi(s,a)$; $\ q_\pi(s,a)=\sum_{s',r}p(s',r|s,a)[r+\gamma v_\pi(s')]$Convert between value types: $v=\sum\pi q$; $q=\sum p[r+\gamma v]$ (needs the model).Ex 3.12–13
Bellman for $v_\pi$$v_\pi(s)=\sum_a\pi(a|s)\sum_{s',r}p(s',r|s,a)[r+\gamma v_\pi(s')]$Check or solve policy evaluation (a linear system for small MDPs, e.g. gridworlds).3.14
Bellman for $q_\pi$$q_\pi(s,a)=\sum_{s',r}p(s',r|s,a)[r+\gamma\sum_{a'}\pi(a'|s')q_\pi(s',a')]$Same as above for action values.Ex 3.17
Optimal values$v_*(s)=\max_\pi v_\pi(s)$, $\ q_*(s,a)=\max_\pi q_\pi(s,a)=\E[R_{t+1}+\gamma v_*(S_{t+1})\mid s,a]$Definition of $v_*$ and $q_*$: best achievable value over all policies.3.15–3.17
Bellman optimality $v_*$$v_*(s)=\max_a\sum_{s',r}p(s',r|s,a)[r+\gamma v_*(s')]$Solve for the optimal value function (non-linear because of the max). Basis of value iteration.3.19
Bellman optimality $q_*$$q_*(s,a)=\sum_{s',r}p(s',r|s,a)[r+\gamma\max_{a'}q_*(s',a')]$Optimal action values; basis of Q-learning (sample version of the max backup).3.20
Optimal policy$\pi_*(s)=\argmax_aq_*(s,a)$; $\ v_*(s)=\max_aq_*(s,a)$Extract the policy: greedy w.r.t. $q_*$ (no model needed), or one-step look-ahead on $v_*$ (model needed).Ex 3.25–27

Chapter 4 — Dynamic programming

NameFormulaWhen to useRef
Policy evaluation$v_{k+1}(s)=\sum_a\pi(a|s)\sum_{s',r}p(s',r|s,a)[r+\gamma v_k(s')]$Model known, evaluate π: sweep all states with the Bellman expectation backup until Δ < θ.4.5
Deviate once$q_\pi(s,a)=\sum_{s',r}p(s',r|s,a)[r+\gamma v_\pi(s')]$Test whether choosing $a\ne\pi(s)$ at one state then following π beats π.4.6
Improvement theorem$q_\pi(s,\pi'(s))\ge v_\pi(s)\ \forall s\ \Rightarrow\ v_{\pi'}(s)\ge v_\pi(s)\ \forall s$Guarantee: if $q_\pi(s,\pi'(s))\ge v_\pi(s)$ for all s, then $\pi'\ge\pi$. Justifies greedy improvement.4.7, 4.8
Greedy improvement$\pi'(s)=\argmax_a\sum_{s',r}p(s',r|s,a)[r+\gamma v_\pi(s')]$Policy-improvement step: take argmax over the one-step look-ahead (needs the model).4.9
Value iteration$v_{k+1}(s)=\max_a\sum_{s',r}p(s',r|s,a)[r+\gamma v_k(s')]$Find $v_*$ directly: one sweep of the max backup replaces a full evaluation. Stop when Δ small, then act greedily.4.10

Chapter 5 — Monte Carlo

NameFormulaWhen to useRef
Greedy from q$\pi(s)=\argmax_aq(s,a)$Model-free policy improvement in MC control (exploring starts): pick $\arg\max_a q(s,a)$.5.1
IS ratio$\rho_{t:T-1}=\prod_{k=t}^{T-1}\dfrac{\pi(A_k|S_k)}{b(A_k|S_k)}$Off-policy: re-weight a behaviour-policy trajectory by $\prod\pi/b$ to evaluate π. Transition probabilities cancel; needs coverage ($\pi>0\Rightarrow b>0$).5.3
IS expectation$\E[\rho_{t:T-1}G_t\mid S_t=s]=v_\pi(s)$Why the ratio works: $\E_b[\rho G]=v_\pi(s)$ (change of measure).5.4
Ordinary IS$V(s)=\sum_{t\in\mathcal T(s)}\rho_{t:T(t)-1}G_t\,/\,|\mathcal T(s)|$Unbiased but can have infinite variance. Use when unbiasedness matters.5.5
Weighted IS$V(s)=\sum_{t\in\mathcal T(s)}\rho_{t:T(t)-1}G_t\,/\,\sum_{t\in\mathcal T(s)}\rho_{t:T(t)-1}$Biased (bias → 0) but far lower variance: the practical choice. Equals the plain average when ratios are equal.5.6

Chapter 6 — TD learning

NameFormulaWhen to useRef
Constant-α MC$V(S_t)\leftarrow V(S_t)+\alpha[G_t-V(S_t)]$Update toward the full return $G_t$; must wait until the episode ends.6.1
TD(0)$V(S_t)\leftarrow V(S_t)+\alpha[R_{t+1}+\gamma V(S_{t+1})-V(S_t)]$Online prediction: update every step toward $R+\gamma V(S')$ (bootstrapping). Works in continuing tasks.6.2
TD error$\delta_t=R_{t+1}+\gamma V(S_{t+1})-V(S_t)$The surprise $\delta_t$; appears in every TD method, critics and n-step sums.6.5
MC error = Σ TD errors$G_t-V(S_t)=\sum_{k=t}^{T-1}\gamma^{k-t}\delta_k$ (V fixed)Relates MC and TD when $V$ is fixed: $G_t-V(S_t)=\sum\gamma^{k-t}\delta_k$.6.6
Sarsa$Q(S_t,A_t)\leftarrow Q+\alpha[R_{t+1}+\gamma Q(S_{t+1},A_{t+1})-Q(S_t,A_t)]$On-policy TD control: target uses the action actually taken next, so it learns the ε-greedy policy's value (safer path in Cliff Walking).6.7
Q-learning$Q(S_t,A_t)\leftarrow Q+\alpha[R_{t+1}+\gamma\max_aQ(S_{t+1},a)-Q(S_t,A_t)]$Off-policy TD control: learns $q_*$ whatever the behaviour policy; target uses max. Risky online (cliff) but optimal asymptotically.6.8
Expected Sarsa$Q(S_t,A_t)\leftarrow Q+\alpha[R_{t+1}+\gamma\sum_a\pi(a|S_{t+1})Q(S_{t+1},a)-Q(S_t,A_t)]$Averages over next actions: lower variance than Sarsa, same cost as Q-learning; can be on- or off-policy.6.9
Double Q-learning$Q_1(S_t,A_t)\leftarrow Q_1+\alpha[R_{t+1}+\gamma Q_2(S_{t+1},\argmax_aQ_1(S_{t+1},a))-Q_1(S_t,A_t)]$Removes maximisation bias: one table picks the action, the other evaluates it.6.10

Chapter 7 — n-step bootstrapping

NameFormulaWhen to useRef
n-step return$G_{t:t+n}=R_{t+1}+\gamma R_{t+2}+\dots+\gamma^{n-1}R_{t+n}+\gamma^nV_{t+n-1}(S_{t+n})$Bridge between TD ($n=1$) and MC ($n=\infty$): $n$ real rewards, then bootstrap. If the episode ends first, use $G_t$.7.1
n-step TD$V_{t+n}(S_t)=V_{t+n-1}(S_t)+\alpha[G_{t:t+n}-V_{t+n-1}(S_t)]$Prediction with n-step targets; update $S_t$ only after $n$ steps (delay). Pick intermediate $n$ for best learning.7.2
Error reduction$\max_s|\E_\pi[G_{t:t+n}|S_t=s]-v_\pi(s)|\le\gamma^n\max_s|V_{t+n-1}(s)-v_\pi(s)|$Guarantee that the expected n-step target is $\gamma^n$-closer to $v_\pi$ than $V$ is (worst case).7.3
n-step Sarsa return$G_{t:t+n}=R_{t+1}+\dots+\gamma^{n-1}R_{t+n}+\gamma^nQ_{t+n-1}(S_{t+n},A_{t+n})$On-policy control with n-step targets; bootstrap with $Q(S_{t+n},A_{t+n})$.7.4, 7.5
Off-policy n-step TD$V(S_t)\leftarrow V(S_t)+\alpha\rho_{t:t+n-1}[G_{t:t+n}-V(S_t)]$Off-policy prediction: scale the update by the ratio $\rho_{t:t+n-1}$ (importance sampling over the n steps).7.9
IS ratio span$\rho_{t:h}=\prod_{k=t}^{\min(h,T-1)}\pi(A_k|S_k)/b(A_k|S_k)$Which steps the ratio covers: $t$ to $\min(t+n-1,T-1)$ for TD, one more for Sarsa.7.10
Off-policy n-step Sarsa$Q(S_t,A_t)\leftarrow Q+\alpha\rho_{t+1:t+n}[G_{t:t+n}-Q(S_t,A_t)]$Off-policy control with n-step targets (ratio starts at $t+1$ because $A_t$ is given).7.11

Chapter 9 — Function approximation

NameFormulaWhen to useRef
Value error$\overline{\mathrm{VE}}(\mathbf w)=\sum_s\mu(s)[v_\pi(s)-\hat v(s,\mathbf w)]^2$The objective: mean-square error weighted by how often states are visited, so frequent states matter more.9.1
On-policy distribution$\eta(s)=h(s)+\sum_{\bar s}\eta(\bar s)\sum_a\pi(a|\bar s)p(s|\bar s,a)$, $\ \mu(s)=\eta(s)/\sum_{s'}\eta(s')$Compute $\mu(s)$ (fraction of time in each state) to weight the error; for episodic tasks from expected visit counts.9.2, 9.3
SGD$\mathbf w_{t+1}=\mathbf w_t-\tfrac12\alpha\nabla[v_\pi(S_t)-\hat v(S_t,\mathbf w_t)]^2=\mathbf w_t+\alpha[v_\pi(S_t)-\hat v(S_t,\mathbf w_t)]\nabla\hat v(S_t,\mathbf w_t)$Idealised supervised update that needs the true $v_\pi$ (unavailable): the template for the practical methods below.9.4, 9.5
General SGD target$\mathbf w_{t+1}=\mathbf w_t+\alpha[U_t-\hat v(S_t,\mathbf w_t)]\nabla\hat v(S_t,\mathbf w_t)$Replace $v_\pi$ by any target $U_t$: $G_t$ (gradient MC, unbiased, true SGD) or a bootstrap target (semi-gradient: biased but faster).9.7
Linear$\hat v(s,\mathbf w)=\mathbf w^\top\mathbf x(s)$, $\ \nabla\hat v=\mathbf x(s)$Hand-crafted features: $\hat v=\mathbf w^\top\mathbf x(s)$, gradient is just $\mathbf x(s)$; one optimum, convergence guarantees.9.8
Linear TD(0)$\mathbf w_{t+1}=\mathbf w_t+\alpha(R_{t+1}+\gamma\mathbf w_t^\top\mathbf x_{t+1}-\mathbf w_t^\top\mathbf x_t)\mathbf x_t$Online semi-gradient TD with linear features; the workhorse update.9.9
Expected linear TD update$\E[\mathbf w_{t+1}\mid\mathbf w_t]=\mathbf w_t+\alpha(\mathbf b-\mathbf A\mathbf w_t)$ ⇒ fixed point where $\mathbf b=\mathbf A\mathbf w_{TD}$Analysis: shows the expected dynamics $\mathbf b-\mathbf A\mathbf w$ and where it settles.9.10
A and b$\mathbf A=\E[\mathbf x_t(\mathbf x_t-\gamma\mathbf x_{t+1})^\top]$, $\ \mathbf b=\E[R_{t+1}\mathbf x_t]$Compute the TD fixed point for a small problem: build $\mathbf A,\mathbf b$ from features, rewards and γ.9.11
TD fixed point$\mathbf w_{TD}=\mathbf A^{-1}\mathbf b$What linear semi-gradient TD converges to: $\mathbf w_{TD}=\mathbf A^{-1}\mathbf b$ (not the minimum of the value error).9.12
Polynomial basis$x_i(s)=\prod_{j=1}^ks_j^{c_{i,j}}$, $\ (n+1)^k$ featuresFeatures from products of state components; $(n+1)^k$ features for order $n$ in $k$ variables; always include a constant feature.9.17
Fourier basis$x_i(s)=\cos(\pi\,\mathbf s^\top\mathbf c^i)$; $\ \alpha_i=\alpha/\|\mathbf c^i\|$Cosine features with integer vectors $\mathbf c^i$; good for smooth functions; use a smaller step size for high-frequency terms.9.18

Chapter 13 — Policy gradient

NameFormulaWhen to useRef
Gradient ascent on $J$$\boldsymbol\theta_{t+1}=\boldsymbol\theta_t+\alpha\,\widehat{\nabla J(\boldsymbol\theta_t)}$, $\ \E[\widehat{\nabla J}]\approx\nabla J$General recipe: climb the performance $J$; any unbiased sample of the gradient will do.13.1
Soft-max policy$\pi(a|s,\boldsymbol\theta)=\dfrac{e^{h(s,a,\boldsymbol\theta)}}{\sum_be^{h(s,b,\boldsymbol\theta)}}$, $\ h=\boldsymbol\theta^\top\mathbf x(s,a)$Discrete actions: probabilities from action preferences $h(s,a,\theta)$; can be made nearly deterministic and can learn stochastic policies.13.2, 13.3
Performance (episodic)$J(\boldsymbol\theta)\doteq v_{\pi_{\boldsymbol\theta}}(s_0)$The objective: value of the start state under $\pi_\theta$.13.4
Policy gradient theorem$\nabla J(\boldsymbol\theta)\propto\sum_s\mu(s)\sum_aq_\pi(s,a)\nabla\pi(a|s,\boldsymbol\theta)$ (no $\nabla\mu$, no $\nabla q$)Gradient of $J$ without differentiating the state distribution — the foundation of every method below.13.5
Expectation form$\nabla J\propto\E_\pi\Big[\sum_aq_\pi(S_t,a)\nabla\pi(a|S_t,\boldsymbol\theta)\Big]=\E_\pi\Big[G_t\dfrac{\nabla\pi(A_t|S_t,\boldsymbol\theta)}{\pi(A_t|S_t,\boldsymbol\theta)}\Big]$Turns the theorem into something you can sample (states and actions from π).13.6, 13.8
Log-derivative trick$\dfrac{\nabla\pi}{\pi}=\nabla\ln\pi$ (eligibility vector)$\nabla\pi=\pi\nabla\ln\pi$: lets you sample actions from π instead of summing over them.§13.3
REINFORCE$\boldsymbol\theta_{t+1}=\boldsymbol\theta_t+\alpha\,G_t\,\nabla\ln\pi(A_t|S_t,\boldsymbol\theta_t)$; discounted: extra factor $\gamma^t$Monte Carlo policy gradient: wait for the episode end, then update with $G_t\nabla\ln\pi$. Unbiased, high variance, slow.13.8, Ex 13.2
Soft-max eligibility vector$\nabla\ln\pi(a|s,\boldsymbol\theta)=\mathbf x(s,a)-\sum_b\pi(b|s,\boldsymbol\theta)\mathbf x(s,b)$Compute $\nabla\ln\pi$ for a soft-max policy with linear preferences: $\mathbf x(s,a)-\sum_b\pi\,\mathbf x(s,b)$.13.9
Zero-mean score$\E_{A\sim\pi}[\nabla\ln\pi(A|s)]=\sum_a\nabla\pi(a|s)=\nabla1=\mathbf0$Why a baseline is allowed: $\E_\pi[\nabla\ln\pi]=0$, so subtracting any $b(s)$ leaves the gradient unchanged.trick

TD error$\delta_t=R_{t+1}+\gamma\hat v(S_{t+1},\mathbf w)-\hat v(S_t,\mathbf w)$ ($\hat v(\text{terminal})\doteq0$)One-step critic signal $\delta=R+\gamma\hat v(S')-\hat v(S)$ (0 for the terminal next state).13.14
Actor update$\boldsymbol\theta_{t+1}=\boldsymbol\theta_t+\alpha\,I\,\delta_t\,\nabla\ln\pi(A_t|S_t,\boldsymbol\theta_t)$, $\ I\leftarrow\gamma I$ ($I=1$ at episode start)Actor–critic: replace $G_t$ by δ: online, lower variance, small bias. Use when episodes are long or continuing.13.12–13.14
Critic update$\mathbf w_{t+1}=\mathbf w_t+\beta\,\delta_t\,\nabla\hat v(S_t,\mathbf w_t)$ (semi-gradient TD(0)); linear: $\nabla\hat v=\mathbf x(S_t)$Semi-gradient TD(0) for $\hat v$ with a (larger) step size β.Ch 9

Gaussian policy$\pi(a|s,\boldsymbol\theta)=\dfrac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\!\Big(-\dfrac{(a-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\Big)$Continuous actions: $a\sim\mathcal N(\mu(s),\sigma(s)^2)$ with separately parametrised mean and std.13.19
Mean and std parameters$\mu=\boldsymbol\theta_\mu^\top\mathbf x_\mu(s)$, $\ \sigma=\exp(\boldsymbol\theta_\sigma^\top\mathbf x_\sigma(s))$Parametrisation: μ linear in features, σ via exp so it stays positive.13.20
Gaussian scores$\nabla_{\boldsymbol\theta_\mu}\ln\pi=\dfrac{a-\mu}{\sigma^2}\mathbf x_\mu(s)$; $\ \nabla_{\boldsymbol\theta_\sigma}\ln\pi=\Big(\dfrac{(a-\mu)^2}{\sigma^2}-1\Big)\mathbf x_\sigma(s)$Gradients of $\ln\pi$ for the mean ($(a-\mu)/\sigma^2$) and std parameters; plug into REINFORCE or actor–critic.Ex 13.4
